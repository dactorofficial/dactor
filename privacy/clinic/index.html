1. Introduction & Fiduciary Scope

Welcome to Dactor ("Dactor", "we", "us", or "our"), Nepal's premier digital outpatient department (OPD) queue intelligence, appointment scheduling, and diagnostic laboratory orchestration platform.

This Clinic & Healthcare Provider Privacy Policy establishes our legal and technical commitment to healthcare facilities (hospitals, polyclinics, diagnostic pathology laboratories, imaging centers), consulting medical practitioners registered with the Nepal Medical Council (NMC), and clinic administrative staff utilizing the Dactor Clinic Android Application (com.dactor.clinic) and Dactor Web Portals.

⚖ Legal Roles (Data Controller vs. Data Processor)

Under the Nepal Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018) and international healthcare standards, the registered healthcare facility acts as the independent Data Controller / Fiduciary with respect to the clinical consultation notes, prescriptions, and health diagnoses of its attending patients. Dactor acts as a secure Data Processor / Technology Infrastructure Provider providing encrypted transmission, queue algorithms, and document access control.

2. Information We Collect from Clinics & Providers

To register, verify, onboard, and maintain the integrity of medical services on Dactor, we collect the following institutional and professional records:

2.1 Facility Institutional Profile

  • Facility Identity: Official registered name, physical street address, municipality, ward number, district, and province in Nepal.
  • Geographic Coordinates: Precise latitude and longitude used exclusively to render your clinic pin on OpenStreetMap for patient geographic discovery.
  • Institutional Documentation: Ministry of Health and Population (MoHP) or local health department registration certificate, Permanent Account Number (PAN) or Value Added Tax (VAT) certificate.
  • Facility Imagery: Exterior clinic photographs, consultation room photos, and institutional logos to assist patients in locating your facility.

2.2 Medical Practitioner Credentials & KYC Verification

  • Doctor Identification: Legal full name, medical specialty, qualifications, languages spoken, and profile photo.
  • Nepal Medical Council (NMC) Licensure: Official NMC permanent or temporary registration number and registration certificate. Dactor validates that all consulting doctors hold active NMC licensure pursuant to the Nepal Medical Council Act 2020.
  • Consulting Schedules & Fees: Regular consulting hours, slot durations, room/cabin assignments, and transparent consultation fees denominated in Nepalese Rupees (NPR).

2.3 Staff Accounts & Device Identity

  • Operator Accounts: Receptionist, billing clerk, and lab technician usernames, mobile numbers, and encrypted authentication tokens.
  • Device Prefix & Concurrency Control: Assigned device prefixes and hardware identifiers ensuring multi-counter desk synchronization without credential collision.

2.4 Diagnostic Laboratory Master Catalogs

  • Investigation Directory: Pathology and radiology test offerings (e.g., CBC, Thyroid, USG, X-Ray, MRI), turn-around time estimates, sample preparation guidelines, and published NPR rates.

3. Patient Information Handled by Clinics via Dactor

While utilizing Dactor's clinical administrative tools, healthcare providers handle sensitive patient records. Dactor provides end-to-end encrypted infrastructure for:

  • OPD Queue & Token Records: Patient names, contact telephone numbers, assigned 10-digit booking codes (e.g., DAC-9821-47), queue positions, and status flags (waiting, in_consultation, completed).
  • Walk-In Registrations: When clinic desk staff register walk-in patients on-site, the staff collects patient identification with informed verbal consent.
  • Diagnostic Reports: Certified laboratory reports, blood chemistry results, and radiology scan PDFs uploaded by verified lab technicians for patient delivery.
  • Itemized Invoices: Consultation charges, laboratory fees, payment receipts (eSewa / fonepay / cash at desk), and invoice timestamps.

4. Operational Workflows & Data Use

Information collected from healthcare providers is processed exclusively for valid operational workflows:

  • Real-Time OPD Queue Synchronization: Calling tokens, coordinating live consultation flow, and dynamically updating patient arrival windows to avoid waiting hall crowding.
  • Doctor Physical Presence Indication: Truthfully communicating whether an attending doctor is physically "In Cabin" or "Away". Deliberate misrepresentation of doctor presence violates Dactor's institutional agreement.
  • Opt-In Early Arrival Alerts: When a doctor finishes early, clinic receptionists may dispatch push notifications strictly to patients who explicitly opted into early calls during booking. Non-opted patients retain their scheduled time.
  • 3-Stage Diagnostic Lab Pipeline: Updating sample progress: (1) Sample Collected, (2) Processing in Lab, and (3) Prepared & Uploaded.
  • Electronic Billing & Fiscal Compliance: Generating auditable electronic receipts aligned with Nepal financial regulations.

5. Doctor-Patient Confidentiality & Nepal Legal Compliance

Healthcare providers utilizing Dactor are bound by the highest legal and ethical obligations of medical secrecy:

5.1 Nepal Individual Privacy Act 2075 (2018) & Privacy Rules 2077

Under Section 3 and Section 28 of the Individual Privacy Act 2075, patient medical history, diagnostic findings, and clinical notes are classified as sensitive personal data. Healthcare providers must maintain total confidentiality. Dactor enforces technical controls, but attending doctors and clinics bear the statutory responsibility under Nepal law never to disclose medical records without explicit patient consent or judicial mandate.

5.2 Nepal Medical Council (NMC) Code of Ethics

Attending doctors must abide by the Nepal Medical Council Code of Ethics (2017). All clinical decisions, prescriptions, and patient advice remain the sole professional responsibility of the attending licensed practitioner. Dactor does not practice medicine and does not interfere with professional clinical judgement.

5.3 Electronic Transactions Act 2063 (2008)

Digital queue slips, consultation bookings, and electronic billing invoices generated through Dactor carry legal evidentiary status as electronic records pursuant to Nepal's Electronic Transactions Act 2063.

6. Architecture, Security & Encryption Standards

We employ state-of-the-art security mechanisms to protect provider infrastructure and patient confidentiality:

  • TLS 1.3 Transport Encryption: All network communication between the Clinic Android app, Web Portal, and cloud infrastructure is encrypted with modern TLS 1.3.
  • Row-Level Security (RLS) Isolation: Multi-tenant PostgreSQL database policies strictly enforce clinic data boundaries. Clinic A cannot access, query, or view records belonging to Clinic B.
  • Cryptographically Signed Private Storage: Uploaded medical documents (lab reports, scans) are stored in private, unlisted cloud storage buckets accessible only through short-lived, time-limited cryptographic signed URLs.
  • Encrypted Offline SQLite Ledger: The Clinic Android application features an isolated local SQLite ledger allowing uninterrupted desk billing during network disruptions, automatically synchronizing upon reconnection.

7. Absolute Zero Data Selling Policy

🚫 Our Binding Zero-Sale Guarantee

Dactor will never sell, rent, broker, lease, or monetize personal, practitioner, or patient health information. We do not sell data to pharmaceutical corporations, marketing agencies, or data brokers.

We partner solely with essential technical infrastructure providers:

  • Cloud Database (Supabase / AWS): Encrypted PostgreSQL storage with strict RLS policies.
  • Push Notifications (Firebase Cloud Messaging - Google LLC): Real-time delivery of priority queue updates.
  • Mapping (OpenStreetMap & MapLibre): Open-source geographical clinic mapping without commercial user tracking.
  • Digital Payments (eSewa / Licensed Nepal Wallets): Authorized payment settlement compliant with Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) digital payment bylaws.

9. Data Retention, Audit Trails & Account Deletion

In compliance with Google Play Store User Data Policies and Nepal laws:

9.1 In-App & Web Account Deletion

Clinic administrators and medical practitioners can initiate permanent account deletion at any time:

  • In-App Deletion: Via Settings → Account Safety → Delete Account.
  • Web Deletion Portal: Via our dedicated Google Play compliant statutory portal at dactorofficial.github.io/dactor/data-deletion.html.
  • Direct Compliance Request: Contact our Data Protection Officer at dactorofficial@gmail.com.

9.2 30-Day Security Grace Period

Upon initiating account deletion:

  1. The facility and doctor profiles are immediately deactivated from public search and booking directories.
  2. A 30-day freeze period allows clinic administrators to cancel the request if initiated inadvertently.
  3. After 30 days, all staff authentication credentials, sessions, and private tokens are permanently erased.

9.3 Medical-Legal Statutory Retention

In accordance with Nepal statutory tax regulations and health facility audit guidelines, finalized fiscal receipts and diagnostic audit logs may be held in immutable, non-active encrypted archives for the legally mandated period before permanent purge.
